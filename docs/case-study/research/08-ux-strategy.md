# 08 · UX Strategy — The Ideal Customer Journey

Persona anchor: **"Priya," 34, Dharampeth.** Working professional or homemaker, wants to lose weight and feel good, hates the idea of a gym floor, found 2Stepz via "zumba classes near me" or an Instagram reel a friend shared. Device: a mid-range Android phone on Jio 4G. Decision unit: often includes a friend ("chal, saath chalte hain") — shareability matters.

## The journey, engineered step by step

### 1 · Arrival (0–3 seconds)
- Page paints fast (LCP < 2.5 s on 4G) — a slow site here *is* the bounce.
- Above the fold: real class in motion (short muted video or striking photo), one headline ("The best hour of your day" energy — final copy in §10), one primary CTA: **Book a Free Trial Class**, with WhatsApp and call icons visible.
- Instant identity check passes: she sees women like her, joyful, in a real Nagpur studio — not stock models.

### 2 · First impression → Interest (3–30 seconds)
- Trust strip immediately under the hero: **4.6★ · Since 2015 · Mrs Fit India 2022 · 10+ formats** — four facts, four seconds.
- Scroll 1: "What we do" — the formats as vivid cards (Zumba, Power Garba, Bokwa, Pilates…), each one line of feeling, not jargon.
- Scroll 2: Madhumita — one strong portrait, three lines of story, titles as badges. The face closes the credibility loop that reviews opened.

### 3 · Trust (30–90 seconds)
- Reviews with names and (where permitted) faces; the "I was nervous at first…" genre placed deliberately — it mirrors her inner monologue.
- Transformation stories with timeframes; community/event photos with real crowds (Garba night!).
- Practical proof: schedule visible, address with map, photos of the actual studio.

### 4 · Excitement → Motivation
- A short cinematic strip (event montage / 46°-heat spirit) sells the *feeling of belonging*.
- "Your first class, step by step" section kills the last fears: what to wear, what happens, no judgment, come with a friend.
- Festival/season hooks when live ("Navratri Power Garba batch — starts Sept 14").

### 5 · Action — three doors, all frictionless
| Door | Design |
|------|--------|
| **WhatsApp** (primary) | Deep link with pre-filled text: "Hi! I'd like to book a trial class at 2Stepz 💃" — one tap, zero typing, lands in a conversation the studio already knows how to close |
| **Call** | `tel:` link; prominent during studio hours ("Call now — we're open till 8 pm") |
| **Trial form** (for the shy) | Name + phone + preferred batch, three fields max; submits to WhatsApp/email; confirmation reassures "we'll message you, no spam" |

Sticky bottom bar on mobile carries all three everywhere (see §11/§14). Membership enquiry is the same mechanics with different pre-filled text — never a locked pricing wall that forces a cold call.

### 6 · Post-action (offsite, but design for it)
- WhatsApp auto-reply template: batch options + location pin + "bring a friend free" nudge.
- After the trial: review-request link (Google), and the site's Transformations page becomes the aspiration loop.

## Journey principles

1. **One primary action per screen.** Every viewport answers "what should I do next?" with exactly one emphasized choice.
2. **Trust before ask, at every depth.** Never show a CTA that hasn't been preceded by a proof element in the same viewport or the one before.
3. **Anxiety-first FAQ.** The real questions are emotional ("Will I look silly?") — answer them where they occur, not only on a FAQ page.
4. **Design for the forward.** Sections should be screenshot-shareable (schedule card, offer card) because Priya decides with a friend on WhatsApp.
5. **No dead ends.** Every page bottoms out into the trial CTA + contact strip. The blog's last line is always an invitation.
6. **Second-visit memory.** Returning visitors (checked schedule once already) get the schedule and CTA within one tap — nav order reflects repeat use: Schedule and Contact never buried.
